## So what is this about?

I've been working as a Software Engineer for 8 years. I started my career writing HTML using jQuery, then writing microservices using Lumen, ExpressJS, and finally Golang. Those are just languages; I haven't even mentioned infrastructure topics like CI/CD, Docker, Kubernetes, Argo, etc. But from a career perspective, I feel like I've been stuck in the same role for years.

## Why do I feel stuck?

I've tried to open opportunities at several companies, but I never made it to the offer stage. The main reason is simple: I haven't grinded enough on DSA, system design interviews, and technical interviews in general. I always fail during technical sessions, whether it's coding interviews, live coding, or similar rounds.

Logical thinking is also my weakest point. Even as I write this post, I'm still not sure how to improve it. Until now, I still can't even solve basic multiplication from 4–10 quickly. Poor brain.

## Why doesn't just practicing resolve that?

I believe it would, but the real problem is that I'm never consistent with my night routine.

For example, today I'll practice LeetCode and create a one-month roadmap to solve DSA problems. Then tomorrow, my brain suddenly comes up with a completely different idea.

> "Hmm... how about I build an app while practicing DSA?"
>
> "I don't feel like practicing is really worth it. Let's build a $10K/month app so I don't have to worry about my career anymore."
>
> "This app doesn't feel worth it anymore. How about I create 2D designs and sell them on Etsy?"
>
> "Hmm... how about becoming a trader?"
>
> "The Japanese language is really interesting. I heard Japan needs more human resources. Maybe learning Japanese will help my career."

And then the cycle repeats.

I've been doing this for almost 7 years. I never complete the projects, never accomplish my goals, and never keep my commitments. Kinda sad.

Apparently, this has a name: **Shiny Object Syndrome**. You keep leaving hobbies or projects half-finished. You get a huge rush of excitement whenever a new idea appears. Then, as soon as the hard part starts, your interest disappears, and you're already chasing the next thing.

## Now what?

I feel like I need to focus on only one thing I want to build or accomplish each month. No more jumping between five different goals at once.

After writing this, I'm planning to talk with a psychologist to discuss my condition. I don't know whether this is simply a discipline problem or something deeper, but I think it's worth finding out.
